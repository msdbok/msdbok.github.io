# Personalities and Management Styles

_Adapted from lecture materials by David Root {% cite root2014lectures %}._

Managing technical people is usually taught through an archetype — the analytical,
autonomy-seeking, authority-sceptical "geek" of early-2000s management writing, along with the
familiar stereotypes about bluntness, hobbies and hygiene. Treat that archetype as history rather
than description: Cruz and colleagues {% cite cruz2015forty %} reviewed forty years of personality
research in software engineering and found **no consistent profile**. Technical people are also not
only programmers: the same questions arise in medicine, research and data analysis.

The six pages below give the frameworks a manager will actually meet; each ends with a
*How solid is this?* section saying how far it has been tested.

- **[Paul Glen's "Leading Geeks" framework](glen.html)** — the archetype this area is built on,
  what motivates technical staff, and what AI-assisted work does to the argument.
- **[MBTI](mbti.html)** — type distributions for software engineers, and why they do not support
  building a team from four-letter codes.
- **[Leadership styles](lead.html)** — Goleman's six styles and where their evidence comes from.
- **[Leading in practice](lead_practice.html)** — choosing and combining those styles, with five
  worked cases from incident response to scaling an organisation.
- **[Communication](comm.html)** — constructive versus destructive delivery, listening as a
  technique, and which meetings need a room.
- **[National culture](culture.html)** — Hofstede's dimensions, what they are good for, and the two
  criticisms that carry numbers.
