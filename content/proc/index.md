# Process

A process is what a team actually does to build software: the steps, who performs them, and how
anyone knows a step is finished. This area covers how to define one, how to measure it without
damaging it, and how to choose the shape a project runs in.

Three groups of pages, in the order they are taught:

- **[Basics](basics/)** — where process thinking came from, how to define and measure a process, and
  what measurement does to the people being measured.
- **[Lifecycles](lifecycles/)** — the phase structures a project can run in, from waterfall through
  to the risk-driven spiral, and when each is the right shape.
- **[Frameworks](frameworks/)** — Scrum, XP, Kanban and the rest: reusable structures of roles,
  events and practices, how they scale, and how to choose between them.

If you read only one thing here, make it [process metrics](basics/metrics.md). Choosing what to
measure is a design problem; deciding who sees the result is a management one, and it is the second
that goes wrong.
