# RQ 4 — Scope: Work Breakdown Structure and Estimation

Covers **L4a** (the work breakdown structure) and **L4b** (estimation), part by part. Summaries at [SN L4a](sn4a.html) and [SN L4b](sn4b.html); the
detail is in the [Scope](../scope/) area.

Answer each in a short paragraph, claim first.

## 1. Lecture Questions — L4a

### 1.1 What a WBS is

**1.** Compare the two halves of the 100% rule. Who pays when each is broken, and why does "if in doubt, include it" follow?

**2.** A colleague calls a network of tasks the WBS. What is missing, and why must the WBS come first?

**3.** Why does a Scrum scope tree stop at the story, and what in Scrum is rolling wave?

### 1.2 Orientation

**1.** Why does a sponsor read a deliverable-oriented WBS more easily than a phase-oriented one? Use the ambulance-dispatch system.

**2.** How does "nouns at the top, verbs at the leaves" reconcile Wysocki's case for activities with PMI's warning?

**3.** Why can the grouping at the top of a WBS decide how many managers a project needs?

### 1.3 Product plus enabling work

**1.** Why does a WBS contain elements that are not parts of the product?

**2.** Why does NASA want the WBS to follow the integration strategy, and what goes wrong if it does not?

### 1.4 Building a WBS

**1.** Why are reused vendor code and a risk mitigation both work that belongs in the WBS?

**2.** When do you stop decomposing, and why should depth differ between branches?

### 1.5 Rolling wave

**1.** Why is a planning package more than a placeholder, and why is the replanning itself a work package?

### 1.6 Keeping the WBS alive

**1.** A project has two sponsors and three releases. Why put one dimension in the tree and tag the rest?

## 2. Lecture Questions — L4b

### 2.1 Why estimates go wrong

**1.** Surveys put the typical overrun near a third; Standish says 89%. What should a manager do with each figure?

**2.** Why keep *estimate*, *target* and *commitment* as three separate words?

**3.** The budget is fixed at 600 hours before anyone estimates. What does that imply, and how do you check it?

### 2.2 The map of methods

**1.** Why estimate size before effort, and where on the size → effort → schedule chain does each family of methods sit?

**2.** What separates the six families of estimation methods, and why does that make them easy to combine?

### 2.3 Expert judgment

**1.** Your company keeps a project database nobody can search. Which estimating strategy does that hurt, and why?

**2.** Why do bias fixes change what estimators see instead of asking them to try harder?

### 2.4 Estimating as a group

**1.** Why can matching cards in a Planning Poker round still hide disagreement?

**2.** Which Poker weakness does each rule of Eduardo's Variation target?

**3.** Why is Planning Poker a poor fit for an architecture or infrastructure item, and what would you do instead?

**4.** What is the advantage of story points, and what is the trade-off?

### 2.5 Function points

**1.** Why count functions rather than lines of code?

**2.** In the Workopolis count, recruiter accounts are an external file although recruiters log in. Why, and what changes if you decide otherwise?

**3.** Why convert a count to hours with your own delivery rate, as a range, and why say what the rate covers?

**4.** COCOMO gave Workopolis about 2,500 hours, the team's rate 1,200 to 1,800. Why must a published model be calibrated before you trust it, and why does its effort grow faster than size?

**5.** Why is a function-point count more trustworthy than an effort estimate made from it?

### 2.6 Combining methods

**1.** No history, incomplete requirements: which methods would you combine, and why?

**2.** Why read the spread of independent estimates rather than average them?

**3.** A rule of thumb gives Workopolis about 580 hours, close to the sponsor's 600. Does that agreement support the budget? Why or why not?

## 3. Reading Questions

Cite the reading you used, with a page number where you have one.

### 3.1 Globerson, "Impact of Various Work-Breakdown Structures on Project Conceptualization" (1994)

**1.** Globerson calls the WBS *"the taxonomy of a project"*. What does the metaphor claim that a task list cannot?

**2.** Why can two WBSs with identical work packages demand different organisations and management styles?

**3.** Why design the WBS early but keep it under configuration management, and what two kinds of chaos does Globerson warn against?

**4.** Globerson treats every orientation as valid; PMI insists on deliverables. Reconcile them, and say how the choice affects monitoring and reporting.

### 3.2 Jørgensen & Boehm, "Formal Models or Expert Judgment?" (2009)

**1.** Why, in Jørgensen's account, are formal models little used despite decades of research, and how does Boehm reply?

**2.** The paper's Table 1 shows the two processes sharing most activities. Why does that undercut the claim that models are objective?

**3.** Where does Boehm say a model earns its place even when experts are available, and does Jørgensen accept it?

### 3.3 Longstreet, "Fundamentals of Function Point Analysis" (2004)

**1.** Why are function points independent of technology, and what problem did that solve?

**2.** How can a function-point count improve communication with users and expose scope creep?

### 3.4 Grenning, "Planning Poker" (2002)

**1.** Grenning targeted wasted time and lopsided participation, not bias. How does the game also reduce anchoring?

**2.** Why does Grenning accept less precision at release level?

### 3.5 Jones, "Software Estimating Rules of Thumb" (1996)

**1.** Why do manual estimates run optimistic, and why does an estimating tool not cure it?

**2.** What do Jones's exponents above and below one tell a manager, and why can no universal ratio turn size into effort?
